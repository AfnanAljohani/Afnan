Phase 1 · Business Understanding

Defining the business problem, the decisions the model must support, how success is measured, and the questions the data must answer.

Problem statement

Fresh food forces a trade-off between availability and waste

Panda's store and DC replenishment of short-shelf-life products is driven by uncertain demand. Too much stock ends in markdown, expiry and disposal; too little ends in stockouts and lost sales.

01

Forecast demand

Units sold, as the core input to every replenishment decision.

02

Predict stockout & near-expiry risk

Flag products likely to run out or expire so teams act early.

03

Support replenishment decisions

Order quantities driven by forecast, not fixed rules.

Scope (Plan B): Product · Supplier · Store.

Analytical questions

Descriptive (D) and diagnostic (G) questions

These questions were defined in Phase 1 and answered with evidence in Phase 2.

Descriptive

  • D1 — How do sales and waste vary across categories and stores?
  • D2 — Do promotions change stockout rates?
  • D3 — What is on-shelf availability by category and store?
  • D4 — How much stock is near expiry?
  • D5 — How do suppliers and DCs perform (fill rate, lead time)?
  • D6 — When and for which SKUs do stockouts happen?

Diagnostic

  • G1 — Why do promotion stockouts happen?
  • G2 — Why do waste quantities vary?
  • G3 — Does store traffic explain near-expiry share?
  • G4 — Does supplier fill rate explain availability?
  • G5 — Do open orders and lead times differ by stock state?
