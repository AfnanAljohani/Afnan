Four structural risks matter more than the missing values

Target leakage

Stockout_Flag = 1 exactly when same-day OSA < 90%. Same-day on-hand ÷ units is 0.40–0.55 for 25,398 of 25,413 stockouts.

→ Exclude same-day OSA and cover as predictors.

Class imbalance

2.54% stockouts (≈ 1 : 38). Promotion and non-promotion rates are almost equal.

→ Resample on training data only; use PR-AUC.

Non-nested IDs

Every store appears in all 7 cities, 5 regions and 30 DCs; a SKU appears under ~4 categories.

→ Use location IDs as labels, not roll-ups.

Sparse history

99.67% of store–SKU pairs appear on one day only, so daily lag features cannot be built.

→ Aggregate the grain for the ABT.

Also noted: Temperature_Zone is independent of Category (≈ 33% each zone), and no seasonality, weekday or Ramadan effect is visible.

Reading the statistics

  • Stock, sales and waste quantities move together — inventory is the common driver.
  • Shelf life affects waste non-linearly (a jump at 2 days), so its linear coefficient is near zero.
  • Price on its own has no association with units sold.
  • The full Min / Max / Mean / Median / Mode / Variance / Std / IQR table is computed in the Phase 2 notebook.

What influences waste, demand and stockouts

Open questions for the data owner
  1. Why does one Store_ID appear under several cities, regions and DCs?
  2. Is Store_Footfall a daily store count or a value per record?
  3. Is Stockout_Flag defined as OSA < 90%? How should zero-inventory records be labelled?
  4. Why are 9,825 Units_Sold values 4–22× the sales value ÷ price?
  5. What do the 128,686 records dated after 28 Sep 2026 represent?
  6. Which categories count as fresh, and what near-expiry threshold applies to each?
  7. Can longer, consistent store–SKU histories be provided?
